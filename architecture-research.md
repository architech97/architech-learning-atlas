# Architectural construction research: 15 precedents

## Construction systems, evidence and lessons

Research cut-off: **20 October 2026**. This report completes a bounded public-source research pass across fifteen selected buildings by five architects. It contains original technical interpretation, **202 evidence records**, source/page/sheet locators, inspection limitations and practical lessons. It does not claim every document in every archive has been found.

The purpose is to teach the two specialist roles how architecture is organized, supported, assembled, finished, altered and documented, and how to distinguish verified evidence from a plausible reconstruction. The companion briefs and assessments translate the findings into reusable working methods. Research does not establish engineering approval or construction readiness.

## Main conclusions

1. Visible form and construction are different information. Fallingwater's documented structural mullions, Robie's concealed steel, Gaudí's different ceiling substrates, Ronchamp's roof/wall separation and Gehry's shaped finish supports make this a practical modelling issue.
2. A freeform envelope is several coordinated systems. Heydar, Guangzhou, Bilbao and Disney require independent representations for primary support, geometric support, weather enclosure, finish and performance surfaces.
3. Time is a model parameter. Permit drawings, early proposals, construction sequences, repair proposals, completed conservation and temporary configurations must remain distinct. London demonstrates why support conditions themselves can change during erection.
4. A caption is a claim to verify. Actual Savoye and Ronchamp sheet titles contradict web captions; the Unité elevation's visible stamp contradicts its catalogue caption. Preserve the conflicting values and the evidence used to qualify them.
5. Printed dimensions need meaning, units and provenance. A panel stock size, tower envelope allowance, acoustic-model scale, nominal span and section-sampling interval are not interchangeable.
6. Documented digital methods do not equal available native data. Scanning, CATIA, CNC and physical/acoustic modelling are described in the sources. The original point clouds, native project models, approved shop sets and complete engineering calculations were not delivered here.

## Scope and inspection method

The evidence ledger is the factual core. Each record provides the building, topic, finding, source URL/title, exact locator, method of inspection, claim type, printed dimension if applicable, date/status, confidence, limits and separate implications for the two roles. The five family studies describe the inspected drawings and texts in more detail.

**Visual inspection** means digital source pages or drawing images were actually viewed at available resolution. It does not mean the building was visited, the original archive sheet handled or the construction physically verified. **Text inspection** means source text or an indexed/extracted passage was read; the associated drawing may remain uninspected. **Catalogue only** means the existence/description of an item was checked but its underlying contents were not reviewed. Mixed methods are stated per record rather than flattened into a false binary.

Claim type is separate from access method: **observed** identifies a directly visible relationship or record; **source-stated** attributes a technical claim to its author; **inference** marks a research conclusion. Confidence applies to that narrow claim. Even a high-confidence record may be unsuitable for engineering or fabrication.

No dimension was derived from uncalibrated image pixels. Reported values are printed and legible in the inspected medium, or expressly attributed to readable text; the locator identifies which. Original paper scales are retained as metadata, not used to claim screen measurement accuracy. A published technical illustration is not labelled IFC, shop-approved or as-built without positive evidence.

# Wright buildings: technical evidence and specialist training handoff

Research date: 20 October 2026  
Buildings: Fallingwater; Frederick C. Robie House; Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, New York  
Companion: `technical_evidence_ledger.json` (45 evidence records, 15 per building)

## Result and scope

The evidence is sufficient for **source-labelled analytical models, historical assembly studies and documentation exercises**. It is not sufficient for an authenticated full construction model, fabrication package, reinforcement takeoff or current as-built set.

The useful lesson across these buildings is that a convincing exterior is an unreliable description of construction. Support can be hidden in a window member; a continuous-looking surface can consist of separate structure, cavity, plaster and finish; restored work can resemble the original while incorporating different materials. The modelling and documentation roles should learn to retain those distinctions rather than eliminate them for visual neatness.

This pass went beyond catalogue descriptions. It inspected technical reports, construction photographs, a dimensioned construction-era section, conservation framing diagrams, existing-condition plan reproductions and interior material descriptions. No measurements were taken from pixel distances. Values in the ledger are legible printed values or explicitly reported dimensions, with their original units and limitations.

### How to use the ledger

- `observed` means a fact directly visible in an inspected image or source passage. It does not mean the building was visited or physically measured.
- `source-stated` means the author states the fact. It is not an independent verification of concealed construction.
- `inference` means a research conclusion, such as recognizing unresolved conflicting evidence.
- Confidence refers to the stated evidence, not permission to treat an historical report as a current construction document.
- Modelling and documentation implications are original recommendations for using the evidence. They are not claims that the source supplied missing drawings.

### Technical reading

The structural cutaway and the accompanying engineer’s investigation must be read together: the cutaway establishes relationships, while the written analysis identifies the consequential support role of the living-room mullions. The historical deflection readings and the planned preservation of tilt mean that an automatically levelled model would erase evidence. The later conservation paper establishes that the repair/restoration proceeded, but does not authenticate every detail in the earlier proposal. See FW-01–05.

The 2017 paper is especially valuable for construction interfaces. It describes the upturned structural system and the restored terrace layers, rather than simply identifying “concrete terraces.” It also explains why the irregular stone surface, finish levels and scribed furniture needed coordinated reinstatement. The stone-recording illustration is useful as a documentation method; its tiny individual labels cannot responsibly be harvested as a stone schedule. See FW-06–08 and FW-15.

Metal conservation adds a second form of assembly evidence: repaired original window fabric and replacement stair-support material are different intervention classes. They should not share one undifferentiated “historic steel” label. The paint and scanning accounts likewise distinguish an analysed finish from a visible current appearance, and knowledge that a scan exists from possession of usable scan data. See FW-09–14.

### Modelling handoff

Create a relationship-first study with separate classes for support, primary beam, secondary joist, soffit, finish/deck assembly, structural window member and operable hatch. The initial model should have a source status on each class and a separate condition-state parameter. Where a connection or thickness is unknown, retain a placeholder or an explicitly schematic representation.

For interior work, prioritize the stone-to-joinery and inside-to-outside interfaces. A smooth generic stone material is adequate for distant visualization only; it cannot support a conservation removal/reinstatement exercise. A detailed study needs stable stone identifiers and local elevations. Do not make an artificial regular stone pattern from the survey grid.

### Documentation handoff

Prepare an original, labelled load-path explanation and an assembly section with unknowns visibly marked. Keep the source’s repair-proposal status beside the proposed cable diagram. A conservation schedule should distinguish surviving fabric, repair, replacement, reconstruction and unknown. A separate source request should seek the final repair package, the full measured conservation survey and scan-control information. None is established as openly delivered here.

### Specific limits

Room-by-room geometry, complete floor elevations, original/current reinforcement schedules, membrane details at every edge, current mechanical systems and exact hatch fabrication are unresolved. The public paper’s room-elevation sample cannot supply those missing details. Neither historic deformation values nor a schematic engineering cutaway should be used to claim present survey accuracy.

### Technical reading

The HABS account is useful for spatial organization and printed interior dimensions; the CMP adds local framing and restoration exceptions. RH-01–03 and RH-11–13 therefore carry a different evidential purpose from RH-04–10 and RH-14–15. Their different floor naming and dates must travel with the data.

The inspected framing plans and cross-section demonstrate that soffits, room height and structural layout need to be coordinated. The prow and window-wall details also discourage treating apparently unsupported glazing or masonry as structurally independent. The roof account distinguishes original form from later replacement layers. The glazing terminology requires material precision, while the reconstructed inglenook-related work requires provenance precision.

There is an important **internal source inconsistency** in the CMP: the secondary-channel total and its two room subtotals do not agree (RH-05). The correct training response is a recorded query. Repeated publication of a number does not resolve its inconsistency.

### Modelling handoff

Organize construction by zone and level, with a crosswalk between HABS and CMP level names. Distinguish structural systems from visible finishes. A model can accurately depict an oak finish while assigning its substrate incorrectly; material appearance is therefore not a substitute for assembly classification.

Use framing direction and support relationships as constraints before refining room proportions. At the prow, maintain room enclosure, glazing and concealed support as separate coordinated objects. Use detail-level parameters only where the report establishes the dimension and what it measures. Nominal channel depth is insufficient for fabricating a historic steel section.

The building also offers a good test of model scale discipline. Overall extent, room relationships, assembly depths and trim profiles belong to different resolution levels. The prose “overall” dimension cannot safely calibrate every wall of a reproduced plan whose endpoints are not defined.

### Documentation handoff

Develop a drawing register that pairs each main-level framing view with its ceiling and interior-elevation counterparts. A finish/joinery schedule should record exact material names, whether the size is a width, height or square section, and whether the fabric survives or was reconstructed. Keep the historical roof states distinct.

When a general historical description and a local detailed account differ, preserve both with their scope. Do not replace all floors with one generic construction simply to make the model consistent. The correct documentation can state a general rule and list local exceptions.

### Specific limits

Complete original shop drawings, weights and full profiles of the steel sections, fastening/connection geometry, current service runs, room-specific joinery fabrication and exhaustive restoration details are not established. The all-house takeoff is blocked by both missing source geometry and the identified joist-count discrepancy. No present-day structural or fire-resistance certification follows from the historical descriptions.

## 4. Reusable training requirements

### For Modelling specialists

1. **Declare the model’s purpose and date.** An explanatory diagram, measured survey model and fabrication model have different evidence thresholds.
2. **Build relationships before appearance.** Test the route from load-bearing element to support and from circulation space to adjacent rooms before adding surface detail.
3. **Separate visible material from substrate.** Represent finishes, framing, cavities and repair layers independently when the evidence distinguishes them.
4. **Store dimension semantics.** Each value needs its units, endpoints, scope, source date and whether it is nominal, approximate, measured or intended.
5. **Preserve uncertainty.** Unknown is a valid value. A plausible default must remain explicitly assumed and must not enter an authenticated quantity or fabrication output.
6. **Use temporal states.** Keep original intent, historical observed condition, proposal, completed intervention and current survey apart.
7. **Set a modelling stop line.** Stop automatic detail propagation where the evidence changes quality, such as unreadable images, missing connections or conflicting counts.

### For Documentation specialists

1. **Give every claim a locator.** Record PDF page, printed page and figure/sheet label together when available.
2. **State what was inspected.** Reading a finding aid is different from viewing the drawing, and viewing a drawing is different from verifying the built work.
3. **Preserve provenance through reproduction.** A plan copied into a report retains its original author/date/status; the host report does not convert it into a new survey.
4. **Issue conflicts visibly.** Do not silently reconcile dimensional, material or quantity disagreements. Specify the further evidence that could resolve them.
5. **Coordinate interfaces.** Link interior elevations, structural sections, ceiling layouts, materials schedules and conservation phases at shared objects.
6. **Separate scope from completeness.** A detailed published page may be excellent local evidence while remaining an incomplete set.
7. **Respect reuse rights.** The handoff contains original summaries and links. Copyrighted source scans and page renders remain private working research; no new reproduction permission is implied.

## 5. Evidence-based exercises and answer notes

### Exercise A: Fallingwater assembly and condition

**Task:** Using FW-01–08, make an original component diagram and a terrace/interior interface section. Tag known relationships, historical measurements, proposed intervention and unknown dimensions. Explain what would be lost by forcing every terrace horizontal.

**Answer notes:** The diagram must keep the support/member hierarchy and structural glazing relationship. The section must distinguish soffit/structural depth from finish/waterproofing layers. Historical sag must carry its dated condition status, and the repair proposal cannot be presented as a verified final connection design. The stone grid is a recording aid, not a proven design module. Accurate geometry remains blocked where full survey and detail data are missing.

### Exercise B: Robie interior coordination and source conflict

**Task:** Produce a coordinated framing/ceiling/trim study for one principal-room zone using RH-03–07 and RH-11–12. Then create a quantity exception for RH-05 and explain which floor-name translation is necessary.

**Answer notes:** A correct response distinguishes primary and secondary member direction, ceiling zones and service/lighting integration. Printed trim dimensions describe different section attributes. The disputed joist count must remain unresolved; an agent that silently chooses the arithmetic sum fails the exercise. HABS and CMP floor labels need a crosswalk rather than an assumed one-to-one textual match.

### Exercise C: Guggenheim parametric model audit

**Task:** Review a proposed model described as “a constant-width, constant-thickness helical concrete ramp, a single glass cap, and a uniform exterior wall.” Use GN-02–08 and GN-12–15 to identify which assumptions require revision and which cannot yet be fixed numerically.

**Answer notes:** The controls must accommodate changing boundaries, local section variation, distinct finish/envelope/roof components and a service-zone interruption. The model needs dated replacement/repair layers. The exercise does not authorize filling missing connection or thermal-section dimensions with conventional values. GN-11 prevents treating an unqualified nominal slope as settled.

### Exercise D: Source-status triage

**Task:** Classify a presentation blueprint, a contractor’s construction article, a 1989 existing-condition plan, a 2000 repair proposal and a 2009 completed-conservation account. For each, list one permissible output and one output it cannot independently substantiate.

**Answer notes:** All can support appropriately labelled analysis; none alone authenticates a full current construction set. The presentation source supports intent, the article supports a documented construction account, the existing-condition plan supports its dated survey state, the proposal supports intended intervention, and the completed account supports reported completion within its stated scope. A source’s age or visual detail does not erase its issue status.

### Evaluation rule

Pass only when the specialist (a) cites source and locator, (b) preserves temporal/status distinctions, (c) separates observation from interpretation, (d) refuses unsupported dimensional precision and (e) identifies the next evidence needed. Attractive geometry without these controls is an incomplete research result.

# Gaudí: technical evidence and training lessons

## What this package establishes

## 1. Sagrada Família: model systems, phases and interfaces

### Research outcome

Use SF-01–SF-04 for interior organization and material systems; SF-05–SF-08 for tower families, support interfaces and sacristy geometry; SF-09–SF-10 for construction/procurement evidence; SF-11–SF-14 for modern engineering and assembly. SF-15 preserves the unresolved milestone discrepancy.

The useful architectural lesson is that a coherent visual language can contain distinct structural materials, construction techniques and design authorship. A single smooth surface model cannot communicate those distinctions. The handoff should contain an evidence-qualified geometric model, a separate system graph and a phase register. None is a substitute for the others.

### Modelling lessons

1. Establish spatial hierarchy before ornamental resolution. Put published datums in a control layer; keep inferred axes, springing points and member sizes visibly provisional.
2. Store each tower as a rule-based family with its own surface-generation assumptions. A generic tapered polygon is an inadequate substitute for an identified ruled-surface family, but an undocumented exact equation would be equally misleading.
3. Represent assembly levels explicitly: building zone → tower/ring → panel → block/connection. The evidence supports the hierarchy more strongly than the individual connection dimensions.
4. At roof/column/tower interfaces, reserve volumes for support branching, circulation and light. A clash-free outer silhouette does not establish that the interior interfaces are coordinated.
5. Maintain separate geometry and material confidence. A well-supported overall level does not make local shell thickness known.

### Documentation lessons

- Keep original design evidence, reconstructed geometry and contemporary engineered work as different document classes.
- An educational cutaway belongs in a source register; it cannot inherit an IFC or as-built label merely because it depicts built work.
- Record publication date, drawing/design date and claimed completion milestone separately. SF-15 is a useful example of why a single date field fails.
- A modern engineer's statement that a fabrication model was made does not mean that model has been supplied. Mark the public evidence as an account of the method.
- Distinguish total envelope allowance from thickness of one material layer when extracting prose dimensions.

### Specific conflicts and gaps

- SF-15 records inconsistent completion milestones. Do not silently choose one year.
- The interior booklet's p.2 introductory apse sentence places the Jesus tower above the apse, whereas the tower booklet's p.4 locates Jesus at the crossing and Mary over the apse. The latter spatial distinction is also consistent with Arup's tower account. Treat the former sentence as an editorial inconsistency, not an alternative layout. [SF9, PDF/printed p.2; SF10, p.4; ARUP, Jesus Cross section.]
- SF4 p.6 contains a caption ambiguity over in-situ versus prefabricated columns. Do not extract a component-wide fabrication status from that caption alone.
- Missing: branch/member set-out, connections, reinforcement and prestressing schedules, panel-by-panel tolerances, current coordinated MEP/fire/access drawings, roof weathering build-ups and native fabrication models.

## 2. Casa Batlló: distinguish design, survival and reconstruction

### Research outcome

BA-01–BA-03 concern the permit-state drawings; BA-04–BA-08 concern interior investigation and craft construction; BA-09–BA-10 concern survey ancestry and spatial change; BA-11–BA-13 add the later rear-façade/courtyard campaign. These should be used together, without blending their dates or purposes.

The key lesson is component-level provenance. A building can have a recognizable historical room arrangement, original surviving finish, later substrate repair, reproduced joinery and recently reinstated external elements in the same view. A model labelled simply "Gaudí original" conceals those differences.

### Modelling lessons

1. Build distinct permit, surveyed and restored states, connected by stable component identifiers. Where an element cannot be matched, record the uncertainty rather than forcing correspondence.
2. Treat a comparative overlay as evidence of change, not a ready-to-trace final plan. Start with relationships that can actually be read at the available resolution.
3. Use assembly-aware interior modelling. Shape, carrier, substrate and decorative finish need separate representation so that a flowing ceiling is not automatically interpreted as a structural shell.
4. Separate flexible sheet/veneer behavior from the geometry of solid rails and skirtings. A parametric finish system should allow room-specific templates and non-repeating curvature.
5. Preserve how much of a reconstructed surface is directly supported by surviving fabric, historical photography or interpolation. Photorealism must not erase that distinction.

### Documentation lessons

- Retain original labels and translations in a source note before standardizing room or floor terminology.
- Keep dimension type explicit: sheet scale, approximate material-stock size, measured installed dimension and inferred dimension are different data types.
- Photograph captions can identify technique or chronology, but they do not automatically establish all details visible in the image.
- A restored finish should have provenance and intervention notes as well as color and material fields.
- Log partial access honestly: BA-12 is a useful lead, but its indexed passage is not an inspected workshop drawing.

### Specific conflicts and gaps

- BA-04 records the 1914/2014 caption inconsistency. Normalization must carry an explicit correction note.
- BA-06 preserves the bilingual width/height discrepancy without inventing thickness.
- The restoration paper's fig.16, PDF p.14 / printed p.111, is a Casa Vicens commercial-catalogue comparison. It must not be filed as a Casa Batlló original detail or used as a direct Batlló dimension source.
- The date references in the publisher preview include different mid-century survey accounts. Do not presume every redrawn plan belongs to one survey campaign without item-level provenance.
- Missing: complete current measured survey, point-cloud registration/accuracy data, full structural load path through retained pre-Gaudí fabric, member/connection details, full envelope sections, service coordination and complete restoration execution records.

## 3. Casa Milà: understand loads without overstating the drawings

### Research outcome

MI-01–MI-02 establish the limited original-design evidence; MI-03–MI-04 cover organization and structural independence; MI-05–MI-07 cover the conservation paper's façade behavior and an uninspected detail; MI-08–MI-12 cover specific basement, interior, attic and envelope systems from the official account.

The important teaching point is the difference between independence and absence of structure. A façade that does not carry the building's floors still requires a self-weight route, lateral restraint and durable connections. A generic "curtain wall" category can hide the very issues that a heritage model and documentation set need to explain.

### Modelling lessons

1. Create independent but related systems for the internal frame, partitions, façade support and ornamental metalwork. Do not derive load-bearing status from wall curvature or thickness in a photograph.
2. Start from court/block/circulation organization; then assign apartment boundaries by state. A room-program label on an early plan does not establish the same present use.
3. Treat the basement ring/radial arrangement as a topology problem first. Nominal prose dimensions do not justify reconstructing each steel member.
4. Keep attic roof support and occupied-floor ceiling systems separate. Never use the same generic curved mesh for every element that looks sculptural.
5. Add condition, inspection date and repair/replacement fields independently of geometry. Historical pathology is neither a current inspection nor an instruction to duplicate deterioration.

### Documentation lessons

- Do not describe MI-07 as a visually inspected detail. It is located, attributed and relevant, but still awaiting page-level examination.
- Do not read an omission in an early plan as proof of permanent absence. Explain the source's phase and purpose.
- Document drainage and fixing interfaces as carefully as the visible railing pattern.
- Avoid spreading a front-façade material description onto rear or courtyard elevations without evidence.
- Follow the exact custodian's permissions for digital reproductions. Underlying artwork age does not make a restricted reproduction unrestricted.

### Specific conflicts and gaps

- A differing scale transcription is flagged by the prior catalogue. The original sheet was not accessible here, so the conflict remains open. No image scaling or original-sheet measurements were performed.
- CSIC's PDF returned service errors/timeouts. Indexed text and the publisher abstract were inspected, but fig.7 itself was not. Its search-index numeric description is deliberately excluded from the verified dimensional evidence.
- The operator's illustration CDN was not available for direct image inspection. No substitute image has been passed off as an official original.
- Missing: inspected high-resolution original plan/elevation sheets, complete measured architectural sections, balcony and façade anchorage details, structural member schedules, attic thrust/restraint details, complete current interior/MEP/restoration documentation and current condition maps.

## 4. Evidence-based training exercises

These are proposed exercises, not additional claims about the buildings. They can be assigned after reviewing the ledger and linked sources.

### Exercise A: reject an attractive but unsupported parameter

**Input:** SF-01 and SF-14. A model draft uses a printed overall envelope allowance as the thickness of its outer skin.

**Modelling task:** Propose an editable hierarchy of controls and unresolved parameters. Do not invent missing material thicknesses.

**Documentation task:** Write a parameter provenance note that distinguishes the source's scope from the draft's assumption.

**Answer notes:** Preserve the nominal overall control, separate each functional layer/clearance, mark unknown thicknesses, and require the responsible technical detail before fabrication use. Passing requires both numerical restraint and clear evidence ancestry.

### Exercise B: reconstruct a room without erasing its history

**Input:** BA-01–BA-04 and BA-09–BA-10. A request asks for one "original Casa Batlló plan."

**Modelling task:** Propose the minimum state structure and match confidence needed before tracing.

**Documentation task:** Produce a short source register with design/submission, survey and restoration-state categories; identify the erroneous caption date.

**Answer notes:** The correct deliverable is an explicitly dated interpretation, not a blend of sources. Keep source geometry segregated until correspondence is established. Do not infer precise dimensions from a small overlay or use a nominal scale declaration to calibrate an arbitrary screen image.

### Exercise C: compare similar ceilings with different evidence

**Input:** BA-05 and MI-09.

**Modelling task:** Create two conceptual assembly schemas using only supported layers; flag all missing thicknesses and fixings.

**Documentation task:** Explain why copying one hidden construction detail to the other building would exceed the evidence.

**Answer notes:** Similar appearance is not evidence of identical concealed construction. Passing requires different supported carrier/substrate systems, separately identified finish geometry, and no invented structural capacity or suspension spacing.

### Exercise D: explain façade loads and durability

**Input:** MI-04–MI-07 and MI-11.

**Modelling task:** Draw a conceptual relationship graph for gravity support, restraint, decorative work and drainage, with unknown nodes.

**Documentation task:** Rewrite an overbroad "non-structural façade" note so that the remaining structural/durability interfaces are visible.

**Answer notes:** A category label must not erase self-weight and restraint. The 1990 report is historical evidence, not a 2026 safety certification. No connection dimensions may be accepted from a machine description of an unviewed figure.

### Exercise E: distinguish restored appearance from material survival

**Input:** BA-07, BA-12–BA-13 and SF-12.

**Modelling task:** Define component metadata for original surviving, reconstructed, reproduced, replaced and unverified elements.

**Documentation task:** Specify the minimum supporting evidence for each category and a procedure for unresolved conflicts.

**Answer notes:** A visually seamless result may legitimately contain several provenance states. Passing requires source/locator links at component level and refusal to relabel reconstruction as surviving original evidence.

## 5. Recommended handoff format and acceptance checks

This is an original proposed workflow for the two specialist roles:

- Supply each evidence record with a stable ID and retain it on every dependent model parameter or note.
- Use a source-date/state matrix before modelling. Include unresolved conflicts rather than silently resolving them.
- Give every dimension one of four statuses: printed exact, printed approximate, measured from authenticated survey, or inferred. For this research, only the first two have been accepted, and neither confers construction authority.
- Separate visual observation from source-authored explanation. Material and structural claims usually need the latter even when a photograph has been inspected.
- Require a legible section/detail before generating buildable geometry. Absence of evidence is a visible gap, not a prompt to complete the detail by habit.
- Never label an image of a model or scan as delivery of its native data.
- For internal training, evaluate whether the specialist maintains uncertainty through to the final schedule and drawing note, not just in an introductory disclaimer.
- Before any external sharing, review rights, intended use and recipients. This package contains original summaries and links; reproduction permissions for source drawings remain separate.

# Technical study: three Zaha Hadid projects

This report records inspected evidence and original training implications. It is a research interpretation, not a structural design or construction instruction. The associated ledger contains the precise source and locator for each finding.

## Heydar Aliyev Center

### What was actually inspected

- Both pages of the [ZHA outline specification](https://cdn.archilovers.com/projects/270b7a85d4954dd185b2f5abc7cae8d2.pdf), read as rendered pages and checked against extracted text.
- The architect-supplied [site plan and longitudinal section](https://www.archdaily.com/448774/heydar-aliyev-center-zaha-hadid-architects/5285282ae8e44e222500015a-heydar-aliyev-center-zaha-hadid-architects-site-plan-plus-section), including its two legends.

The drawings establish a wider site/underground system: landscape and parking, linked circulation, differentiated entrances and a separate utility centre. The specification establishes distinct primary frame, external panel support/weather barrier, and suspended interior lining systems. The resulting visual continuity must not be interpreted as a monolithic structural or waterproof surface. Specification p.1 also distinguishes nominal panel-joint types and gives component-size ranges; p.2 identifies an adjustable secondary support arrangement for the inner lining. These are outline requirements rather than complete shop drawings.

### What Modelling should learn

Build a hierarchy before a smooth shape. Begin with spatial zones and circulation, a reference surface, structural support, weather envelope and internal finish. A change to the reference surface should trigger review of all affected systems; it should not silently remesh every downstream object and erase component identities.

Maintain two geometrical intentions: the visible form and the actual offset assemblies needed to support, seal, drain, access and maintain it. The appearance of a continuous surface does not mean that all offsets remain well behaved at tight curvature or transition zones. A model should identify those difficult regions for coordinated detailing rather than disguise them with a generic material.

Treat unique nodes and panels as identity-bearing objects. An educational model can demonstrate a naming and revision scheme without pretending to reproduce the project’s manufacturing database. Imported dimensions should retain source, page, units and scope. A published diameter range does not authorize choosing member sizes, wall thicknesses or structural capacities.

### What Documentation should learn

Make interface responsibility visible. A useful detail package identifies which source controls the frame, panel support, weather layer, cladding, interior support and service penetrations. A visually attractive section with unnamed layers is an unresolved coordination view.

Keep performance statements separate from physical build-ups. A glazing type needs both, together with the missing interlayer, edge and fixing information. Where source notation is awkward or appears internally inconsistent, preserve an unresolved query rather than “correcting” it silently.

### Limits and exercise

No full member register, installed tolerance survey, complete lining performance assembly or fabrication file was inspected. Exercise: draw an information-only assembly tree with seven interfaces, showing each as verified, inferred or unresolved. A passing answer separates rainscreen from weather barrier, does not invent insulation thickness, and identifies the need for supplier/engineer details.

The 0.00 m plan exposes public/backstage separation; the 20.50 m plan explicitly labels several voids above tall working spaces. The A–A drawing makes the outer enclosure, foyer balconies and auditorium/service volume separately legible. The structural abstract identifies a steel folded-grid system supported on the interior concrete, including cast nodes at ridge intersections. Start with operational volumes and routes, not only the two outer forms. Tall stage/rehearsal spaces and their voids constrain slabs, circulation, plant and support. An attractive exterior generated without those reserves can be impossible to coordinate even if its surface is mathematically clean.

Use separate representations for exterior geometry, structure and room acoustics. A triangulated envelope and a flowing internal acoustic surface have different functions and evaluation methods. A watertight mesh is not automatically an acoustic analysis model. A visually plausible panel is not evidence of its thickness, backing or fixing.

Keep the dated A–A section as a specific design state. The 2011 publication date does not turn a 2005 drawing into a final as-built record. Educational reconstruction should cite that state and label unresolved differences.

### What Documentation should learn

A room/space schedule must distinguish occupied rooms from voids. Level labels belong to the source datum and must not be converted to a site elevation without the datum definition.

Use the interface sections to frame precise requests: material key, movement provision, moisture control, fire-stopping, acoustic continuity, fixing type and responsible package. Do not fill missing labels with a plausible-looking standard detail and present it as the project’s original design.

Exercise: produce a route-and-void diagram from the two inspected plans, then create a six-question interface RFI based on gallery 24. Passing work uses the printed levels, distinguishes observation from inference, identifies GRG correctly and does not claim exact component dimensions from the uncalibrated image.

## London Aquatics Centre

### What was actually inspected

Both pages of the [structural steel award account](https://www.steelconstruction.info/images/a/af/2010_-_London_Aquatics_Centre.pdf), including construction photographs, and the [legacy longitudinal section](https://cdn.archilovers.com/projects/664da4ac-f2bf-4ead-a551-ab8555204422.pdf) were visually read. The [concrete contractor’s account](https://www.morrisroe.co.uk/project/london-aquatic-centre) was read for its modelling/formwork process.

The roof paper describes longitudinal fan trusses feeding transverse supports, a different initial restraint condition during erection, and temporary tying before the permanent plaza slab was available. It also identifies corrosion-sensitive connection treatment and fabrication/erection ranges. The section establishes roof depth and pool/occupied volumes without supplying a complete technical assembly.

### What Modelling should learn

Time is part of the structural model’s meaning. “Built geometry” alone cannot represent the conditions while the roof is being assembled, raised, restrained or transferred to permanent bearings. The training model should distinguish erection, Games, conversion and legacy states, with element status and support assumptions explicit.

Separate component size from assembly size. A fabricated member, a liftable erection assembly and the whole roof are different objects with different identifiers and coordination responsibilities. Published dimensional ranges help identify this hierarchy; they do not define a safe lifting plan.

Treat movement as an interface requirement. A model showing the same restraint at both ends in every phase contradicts the inspected construction account. The appropriate response is a coordinated assumption register and specialist review, not an assistant-generated structural calculation.

### What Documentation should learn

A drawing must state its configuration and purpose. The temporary seating arrangement and completed legacy façade belong to different states. A section labelled “legacy” cannot be mixed into an Olympic package without an explicit explanation.

Documentation should also keep appearance, corrosion protection and connection performance distinct. A painted member may have a specific tested faying-surface requirement; a finish schedule alone cannot establish slip resistance.

### Limits and exercise

No complete erection calculation, bearing specification, pool waterproofing set or diving-platform reinforcement package was inspected. Exercise: create a four-state information matrix with support-condition fields, temporary elements and missing approvals. Passing work does not claim engineering verification and explicitly marks the construction-stage transfer of restraint.

## Common lesson

Across the three projects, the most reusable lesson is to preserve distinctions: visual form versus load path; cladding versus weather barrier; lining versus enclosure; component versus erection assembly; source drawing versus built state; modelling output versus performance approval. The learning objective is disciplined technical interpretation and coordination, not imitation of a famous building’s appearance.

## 1. Villa Savoye

### Architectural model: distinguish route, support and enclosure

Start with a **topological model**, using S01-S04. Establish the service/arrival level, raised residential level, terrace and upper route as distinct spatial systems. Link the route to its destinations before shaping its surfaces. A visually persuasive ramp that reaches the wrong landing is a failed reconstruction.

The model should maintain independent categories for supports, floors, infill, glazing, roof screens and joinery. The construction photograph and CMN account provide a useful test of this decomposition (S05-S08). Do not let a clean, white finished surface collapse the classification of everything behind it. Equally, the explanatory “free plan” must not be turned into an unsupported assertion that every slab uses the same structural action.

**Conceptual load-path teaching exercise:** trace floor support to columns, then explicitly stop where the inspected evidence stops. Mark foundations, lateral stability, reinforcement and unverified beam/slab junctions as unresolved. The correct student output includes those boundaries. It is not improved by drawing plausible foundations from memory.

### Interior construction deserves its own model

Use S09-S12 to build an interior assembly hierarchy rather than populate empty rooms with generic furniture. Separate fixed storage, moving fronts, sanitary forms, work surfaces, fixtures and service runs. Geometry that divides a room without reaching the ceiling should not be automatically classified as a full-height wall.

A useful documentation exercise is to correlate a historical room photograph with plan, interior elevation and reflected-ceiling views. The student must identify what each source can actually establish: component presence and relation may be strong; material substrate, dimensions, colour and fixing methods may remain unknown. Modern replacement products should never be inserted as if they were historical evidence.

### Envelope and assembly limits

The material categories are better established than their detailed build-ups. The research did not locate a verified complete sequence of masonry, render, paint, insulation, waterproofing and finish thicknesses. The roof/terrace interfaces warrant particular attention because S13 records performance and restoration history. Do not “solve” that history by attributing a present-day roof specification to the original design.

Create detail placeholders for:

1. Window head, jamb and sill at the infill/frame interface
2. Interior-to-terrace threshold and drainage relationship
3. Roof-light upstand and waterproofing termination
4. Planter interface with accessible roof finish
5. Bathroom finish/substrate/waterproofing junction
6. Joinery fixing and sliding-panel hardware

These are **requested information**, not proposed historical details.

### Dimension and phase controls

S07 records a published design-change dimension; S14 records a published room dimension. Neither authorises scaling every raster or treating a nominal narrative value as a current survey. A student should preserve versioned grids and a dimension source register. Do not combine an earlier plan, a later restoration photograph and a conceptual lecture sketch into a single undifferentiated “as built”.

**Recommended training deliverable:** an evidence-keyed spatial model, one circulation section, one interior elevation and six unresolved-detail callouts. Acceptance requires correct lower-level classification, independent frame/infill objects and a visible original/restored-state distinction.

## 2. Unité d’Habitation, Marseille

### Solve the three-dimensional apartment logic first

U01 and U02 should be used together: the section establishes vertical relationships while the plan establishes furniture, storage and circulation. Model a small interlocking test assembly before propagating it through the building. Run connectivity checks from corridor to entry, stair, living zone, sleeping zone and loggia.

U03 is especially valuable because a research diagram records **compatibility problems**, not merely a finished ideal. A capable Modelling specialist must preserve alternatives and detect incompatible module combinations. It should not convert every published axonometric into a repeated final unit.

U06 prevents the opposite error: extending a residential repeat through communal, entrance and rooftop conditions. Use a global level/zone structure that distinguishes local apartment levels from building storeys. Match room and unit IDs across plans, sections, schedules and model objects.

### Separate primary frame, apartment enclosure and services

U07-U09 support an assembly hierarchy, but the inspected sources do not supply a complete engineering model. A conceptual gravity-path diagram can show the relationship between repeated structure and the primary supporting system; reinforcement, connection forces, foundations and lateral resistance remain outside the verified evidence.

For acoustic coordination, preserve interfaces instead of joining all solids into one continuous shell. The existence of an isolation aim is not an acoustic rating. The exact archive route in U15 is more useful than inventing a generic resilient layer: request the installation diagrams, material quantities, tests and later corrective records together. The same principle applies to services: a cabinet or threshold may also be an access element, so spatial modelling should include operable and maintainable states.

### Fabrication evidence: surface treatment can be technical

The [entrance-wall formwork sheet FLC 25217](https://lecorbusier-worldheritage.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/13-unite-habitation-plan-coffrage-mur-facade-est-hall-entree.jpg) is stronger construction evidence than a general photograph. U04 records its relationship between finished relief, temporary forming components and a companion detail reference.

A training exercise should model those three things separately:

- The permanent concrete surface
- The temporary forming/insertion element
- The drawing reference needed to manufacture or position it

Do not interpret drawing colour as evidence that the corresponding finished concrete was painted. Likewise, do not convert a formwork dimension into a finished-component dimension without checking the convention.

### Interior lessons and reconstruction status

U10-U13 provide bounded study cases for mixed-material fabrication, movement and service access. They come from a museum reconstruction and educational description. That status should be displayed on any derived component family. A reconstruction can be rigorous and still be a different evidentiary object from the original installed fabric.

Use the interior study to teach component interfaces and clearance logic, not to produce an unverified shop drawing. Required checks include stair/guard coordination, cabinet movement, removable or lifting panels, heating access, and which visible finish belongs to which substrate. Retain unknown hardware, thicknesses and tolerances as unknowns.

### Quarantine conflicting quantities

U14 records conflicting apartment counts, type classifications, the educational account’s factory-assembly wording and a numerical inconsistency in its stated area total. The report does not select a corrected apartment count or total area. Those require an authoritative unit schedule and measurement convention. The same caution applies to the “bottle-rack” metaphor: it is useful for conceptual organisation, but is insufficient evidence for the literal handling of complete finished apartment pods.

**Recommended training deliverable:** a three-level interlocking test model, coordinated apartment plans/section, a primary/secondary assembly diagram, one formwork-to-finished-surface example and a contradiction log. Acceptance requires no unsupported full-pod assembly claim and no model-generated quantity schedule derived from the disputed totals.

## 3. Ronchamp

### Model geometric appearance and construction separately

R01-R04 and R11 establish why one solid sculptural mass is an inadequate teaching model. Use separate systems for wall shape, infill, supporting frame, roof, openings and daylight separation. A conceptual load-path diagram should be consistent with that hierarchy while leaving actual support coordinates and connections unresolved.

The printed roof values in R03 are useful source statements. They are **not** permission to create a uniform-offset two-surface roof and assert that every part of it is dimensionally correct. The measurement convention, local curvature, ribs, bearing locations and weathering package require drawings not inspected here.

For the south wall, avoid mapping all apertures with one generic rectangular window family. R09 shows why at least the interior reveal, glazing plane and outer aperture must be distinct parameters. These should be attached to stable opening IDs so that a revised pane or splay does not lose its documentary trace.

### Projection and chronology matter as much as shape

R05 gives a reliably legible early drawing date. R06 identifies a non-trivial projection convention. These are safeguards against common reconstruction errors: treating an early elevation as completion evidence, or aligning an image to a cardinal view when its own title specifies another relationship.

Use R08 as a topology and cutaway check. A drawing that deliberately removes the roof is not evidence of a construction stage. The modelling system should record whether an absent object is omitted representationally, unbuilt in that design state, demolished, or simply undocumented.

### Materials, colour and craft

R07 provides a legible material key, but a material category does not equal a layered specification. Link each resolved leader to its element; leave unreadable leaders unresolved. Preserve distinctions between exposed surfaces, applied finishes, transparent materials and separate doors or stone elements.

R10 and R12 require equally careful treatment of artwork and craft. Geometry, optical material, pigment/artwork and fabrication method should occupy separate data fields. A photographed or scanned colour is not a calibrated specification. A named craft contributor also should not be erased by assigning every object to a generic architect field.

Useful documentation exercises include a numbered aperture elevation, a material-key concordance, a door/bench/glazing request register and a section through an unresolved roof-support interface. None should invent glass mounting, pivots, bench fixings or waterproofing layers.

### Condition and intervention evidence

R13 and R14 should be stored as time-stamped diagnostic and intervention evidence. They do not supply a current structural safety assessment. Keep observed condition, consultant diagnosis, repair scope and verified close-out state distinct. This is particularly important if a learner is comparing pre-restoration and recent photographs.

**Recommended training deliverable:** an evidence-keyed spatial/cutaway model, an opening-indexed south-wall study, a conceptual support diagram and a phase register. Acceptance requires separation of south-wall construction from other infill, explicit projection conventions and no unsupported roof reinforcement/foundation detail.

### Next archive request

Start with [Ronchamp’s inventory](https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr/archive/architecture-chapelle-notre-dame-du-haut-ronchamp-france-1950/): Q1-1 items 13-15 and 97-117; Q1-2 items 104-115 and the relevant work-account ranges identified in R15. The execution register should guide requests for particular roof, wall, glazing and joinery sheets. Resolve dates at original-document level rather than choosing between inconsistent overview narratives.

## Reusable training and documentation protocol

1. **Establish source identity.** Preserve archive ID, original sheet number, title, date, revision, scale and view convention separately. An archive accession number is not an issue date.
2. **Classify access depth.** Use visually inspected, text-inspected and catalogued-only states. Store claim type independently.
3. **Choose the historical state.** Original proposal, revised design, construction, restored fabric and current survey must never merge silently.
4. **Build topology before metric detail.** Validate spatial connections and level relationships before selecting precise geometry.
5. **Separate systems.** Structure, enclosure, finish, joinery, services and temporary construction are different object classes even when visually adjacent.
6. **Carry uncertainty into outputs.** Unknown thicknesses and illegible dimensions remain null; they do not become standard-library defaults labelled as facts.
7. **Check every dimensional claim.** Record values exactly as printed, with locator and measurement convention. Do not measure compressed reproductions using a nominal printed scale.
8. **Coordinate views and schedules.** Every claimed detail should resolve to an object and a source; every source conflict should remain discoverable from the affected output.
9. **Test arithmetic and provenance.** Official educational material still needs consistency checks. Confidence is per claim, not per institution.
10. **Keep training outcome bounded.** These sources support analytical exercises now. A construction-ready reconstruction requires additional authenticated detail, survey and engineering evidence.

## 2. Building-specific training use

### Guggenheim Bilbao

Use the Architectural Review section series as a coordination exercise: reconstruct relationships among major voids, occupied masses and external ground before adding secondary shapes. The useful deliverable is a diagram with traceable assumptions, not an artificially precise complete building.

For modelling, keep five distinct objects or linked systems: spatial volumes, primary structure, geometry-support framing, environmental envelope and interior finish support. Give each a source/status property. An object derived from a publication image should never silently become a verified structural member when exported.

For documentation, build an interface register around the transitions: building/ground, primary/support framing, support/weather barrier, weather barrier/outer finish, and inner lining/services. A detail can remain an explicit unanswered question. A plausible invented detail is worse training data than an honest gap.

The ledger captures important source disagreements. Reconcile neither the panel quantities nor the material terminology by averaging or majority vote. The appropriate output is a discrepancy record listing source, date, scope and the document needed to resolve it. Contractor marketing claims about digital “firsts” are not used as technical proof.

### Walt Disney Concert Hall

## 3. Dimension discipline

The ledger transcribes a small number of legible values and labels the exact referent. It preserves decimal commas where present. It differentiates spatial dimensions, material thickness, sampling intervals and acoustic performance values.

- Do not derive dimensions from a downloaded image's displayed size or nominal PDF page size.
- Do not extrapolate a printed thickness to other walls, floors or projects.

## 4. Reusable specialist workflow

### Modelling specialist

1. Create a source register before geometry. Record edition, page/sheet, status, view type, inspected medium and confidence.
2. Establish only the datums and relationships supported by evidence. Keep unknown coordinates genuinely unknown.
3. Separate spatial, architectural, structural, environmental and fabrication representations. Shared appearance is not shared function.
4. Make inference visible through object metadata and a dedicated assumptions schedule.
5. Validate relationships across independent views. Record contradictions rather than deforming one view to make it fit another.
6. Maintain identifiers and transformation logs through file exchange. Perform round-trip checks before increasing detail.
7. Stop at an evidence-appropriate level of development. Dense meshes do not create verified information.

### Documentation specialist

1. Classify every source as design study, publication illustration, analytical diagram, construction photograph, issued drawing, shop drawing or survey. The last three require positive verification.
2. Select a view for the question being answered. Do not use the same projection automatically for shape, assembly, reinforcement and setting-out.
3. Link annotations to evidence and distinguish quoted values from derived ones.
4. Schedule interfaces and responsibilities, including unresolved joints, penetrations, tolerances, access and temporary works.
5. Keep chronology and supersession explicit. Separate historical evidence from current code compliance or present condition.
6. Issue a limitations sheet with the training package. State which documents must be obtained before fabrication, structural verification or construction.

# Final synthesis and handoff standard

## What the Modelling role should now be able to explain

The model should express spatial organization, construction hierarchy, support relationships, enclosure layers, interior substrates, fabrication identity and project state independently. It should carry evidence links and unknowns rather than replace absent information with plausible defaults. The most important test is whether the model remains understandable and traceable when rendered appearance is removed.

## What the Documentation role should now be able to explain

A useful drawing package answers a question through an appropriate view, datum, annotation and revision state. It coordinates interfaces and responsibilities, records source conflicts, distinguishes an existing record from a proposed resolution, and makes missing information actionable. A polished sheet with unverified dimensions is a failed outcome.

## Review gates before further use

- **Research-study gate:** source links, locators, dates, confidence and unresolved conflicts are present.
- **Model gate:** every consequential element has evidence and status; unsupported detail remains schematic or unknown.
- **Coordination gate:** interfaces across structure, architecture, envelope, interiors, services and temporary works are identified with owners and open questions.
- **Production gate:** authorized measured geometry, approved design, current regulations, engineering verification, tolerances and issue/revision control are obtained. This research does not satisfy that gate.
- **Learning gate:** the two specialists answer the exercises independently and are assessed against the separate rubric. No training success is asserted before that review.

## Remaining acquisition priorities

For all fifteen, the highest-value next records are authenticated drawing registers, full-resolution dimensioned plans and sections, current measured surveys, engineering and connection details, envelope junctions, room/finish/door schedules, interior fabrication details, services coordination, alteration records and original issue/revision metadata. The individual studies name project-specific gaps. Where access is controlled, request the records from the custodian and establish permitted use; do not infer that a missing public link means the record does not exist.
